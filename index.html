About Council Member David

Thirteen years of civic leadership — now with a seat at the table

Christine E. David has spent two decades as an Atherton resident and thirteen years as an active civic participant — on advisory committees, in nonprofit leadership, and in direct advocacy before the Council. In December 2026, she took the one seat she hadn't yet held.

She helped design the Town Center and library as a Civic Center Advisory Committee member, co-chaired marketing for Atherton Now (the fundraising campaign that made the civic center possible), served as Vice Chair of the Transportation, Bicycle and Pedestrian Safety Committee, served as Vice Chair of the Park and Recreation Committee, sat on the board of ADAPT, and led the Friends of Atherton Library as Chair before her election.

"I have found it difficult to establish real change without being a member of our town council. It is time to bring twenty years of civic commitment to the seat where I can do the most good." — Christine David, 2026 Campaign Statement

She did not run against the institution. She helped to build it — through Atherton Now, through the Civic Center Advisory Committee, through the Friends of Atherton Library. Now she leads it from the dais, carrying forward the same standard of resident-first engagement into every vote.

Every committee. Every meeting. Every fundraising ask. Christine was present in the civic life of this town not because it was convenient, but because she believes — fundamentally — that residents are the rightful stewards of the place they call home.

Park & Recreation Committee

Fighting for Holbrook-Palmer — twice

Christine's Park and Recreation Committee service produced two of the most consequential civic moments in recent Atherton history — both involving threats to Holbrook-Palmer Park, both ending with the park protected.

✓ Park Protected

The Water Capture Facility (2018)

Caltrans offered Atherton $13.6 million to build an underground concrete water-capture facility at Holbrook-Palmer Park — buried 11 feet deep under 6 feet of soil, holding 9 acre-feet of water. The money was real. The pressure to accept it was real.

At the March 7, 2018 Park and Recreation Committee meeting — the very committee Christine sat on — more than 60 residents showed up to oppose the project. Christine was in the room as a committee member, hearing those concerns not as a distant administrator but as a neighbor. The committee's message was clear: the park belongs to recreation, not infrastructure.

By May 2018, the Council directed staff to stop work on the park site. By September, Cartan Field at Menlo College/Menlo School was identified as the alternative. Holbrook-Palmer was preserved.

Principle Over Protocol

The Dog Park Resignation (2019)

Christine joined the Park and Recreation Committee in 2015, contributing to master plan updates and park programming. The committee began studying the feasibility of an off-leash dog area in Holbrook-Palmer Park. Christine did not just attend meetings — she drafted the proposal herself: a detailed 21,000-square-foot dog park near the tennis courts, with cost estimates between $65,000 and $100,000.

The committee voted to recommend the concept to the Council. At the November 2019 Council meeting, Christine arrived expecting a deliberative review. What she got instead was a split vote to remove the dog park from the master plan entirely.

Christine resigned from the Park and Recreation Committee in protest — a principled act that clarified her motivations for ultimately seeking elected office. She did not do so quietly: she immediately began planning how to keep the proposal alive through other channels. That experience is part of why she ran — and why Atherton voters sent her to the Council.

A.D.A.P.T. — Get Ready Atherton

Disaster preparedness: the civic work no one sees until it matters

From April 2021 through June 2023, Christine served on the board of ADAPT — the volunteer organization that prepares Atherton for the disasters it hopes never to face.

ADAPT — the Atherton Disaster and Preparedness Team — is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit formed in 1999 and incorporated under Scott Barnum's leadership in 2008. It partners with Atherton Police, the Menlo Park Fire District, San Mateo County Office of Emergency Management, and state and federal agencies to prepare Atherton's approximately 7,200 residents for major emergencies: earthquakes, wildfires, floods, train derailments, and hazardous material events.

ADAPT organizes its work across 14 neighborhood areas, each with Area Coordinators and Block Captains. It manages Atherton's emergency communication network, maintains a mobile command vehicle and equipment caches, and conducts annual community emergency drills.

Christine held a board seat from April 2021 through June 30, 2023 — serving during the organization's active expansion of community programming, neighborhood coordination, and public education efforts. This is quiet, unglamorous, consequential civic work: the kind that saves lives precisely when it is invisible the rest of the time. As Council Member, Christine continues to champion the partnership between ADAPT, police, fire, and county emergency services.

Friends of Atherton Library

Bringing the library to life, every week

As Chair of the Friends of Atherton Library since 2023, Christine has transformed a well-respected nonprofit board into an active civic institution — and continues this work alongside her role on the Council.

The Friends of Atherton Library is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit founded in 1967 whose mission is to promote community support and use of the Atherton Community Library. Under Christine's leadership, the board has partnered with the Town of Atherton, the Atherton Police Department, and library staff to expand programming, grow membership, and establish the library as a genuine community center for residents of all ages.

The library itself — which the community helped design and fund — has been called "a centerpiece of Atherton, a welcoming oasis and a prime destination for the community" by the AIA jury that gave it a national Library Building Award. Rick DeGolia serves as the San Mateo County Library JPA Governing Board representative on the Friends board — bringing county-level governance expertise alongside Christine's community programming leadership.

On the Council

What Christine is championing in office

These priorities flow directly from what she learned in committees, at community meetings, and as a resident of this town for more than twenty years — and now guide her votes on the dais.

🏘️

Resident voices — genuinely heard

Residents should lead, not just advise — their voices genuinely heard in every significant decision. Christine resigned from the Park and Recreation Committee because the Council dismissed its recommendation without engaging with the community's work. That experience guides how she votes.

🌳

Holbrook-Palmer Park — protected as recreation

Atherton's only public park belongs to its residents for recreation, community events, and open space. Christine has twice participated in defending it against conflicting uses. She will continue to insist that the park's recreational character is the primary constraint on any proposal affecting it.

📚

A living, thriving library

Continued investment in programming, partnerships, and events that make the library Atherton's true community center. The Friends' work is proof of concept — Christine will protect the institution from the dais while continuing to lead the Friends board.

🚗

Pedestrian & bicycle safety

As a former Vice Chair of the Transportation, Bicycle and Pedestrian Safety Committee, Christine brings firsthand knowledge of Atherton's infrastructure needs — prioritizing safe pedestrian routes, crosswalk improvements, and bicycle paths for residents of all ages.

🚨

Emergency preparedness

Christine's two-year ADAPT board tenure is the rare civic credential that addresses Atherton's actual physical vulnerability. She understands how the organization works and will strengthen the partnership between ADAPT, police, fire, and county emergency services.

🤝

Community character & history

Atherton's extraordinary character — its trees, its quiet streets, its neighborly culture, its history — is not an accident. It is the result of deliberate decisions made over decades. Christine will protect it as the defining constraint on every planning decision the Council faces.
